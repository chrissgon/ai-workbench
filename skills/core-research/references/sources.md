# Source tiers and citation rules

## Tiers

| Tier | What | Use |
|------|------|-----|
| 1 | Primary: official documentation, standards bodies, regulators, company filings and pricing pages, public datasets, peer-reviewed research, the original survey or benchmark | Supports a claim on its own; two tier-1 sources make a `fact` high-confidence |
| 2 | Reputable secondary: established press with editorial standards, analyst reports that state their method, maintainers' or vendors' engineering blogs, conference talks with data | Supports a claim; pair with another independent source for key claims |
| 3 | Weak: forums, unsourced blogs, content farms, social posts, machine-generated summaries, marketing copy about competitors | Leads only. Never the sole support for a claim. Cite only to show that a belief circulates, labelled `opinion` |

Paywalled or inaccessible sources: cite only what is visible (abstract, headline figure) and mark `partially accessible`.

## Independence

Two sources are independent when the facts were gathered separately. Signs they are not: same figures to the decimal, same phrasing, one cites the other, both cite the same press release or report. Count dependent sources as one and cite the origin.

## Recency thresholds (defaults; the user can change them in step 1)

| Topic | Flag if older than |
|-------|--------------------|
| Technology adoption, tooling, pricing, market size | 12 months |
| Company facts (funding, headcount, product availability) | 6 months |
| Regulation, standards, specifications | 3 years, unless amended since |
| Established science, history, definitions | no threshold |

## Citation format

`[n] Title — Publisher. Published YYYY-MM-DD | undated. Accessed YYYY-MM-DD. URL. Tier t. Quote: "exact words or figure with unit"`

The quote is mandatory: it is what lets a reader verify the claim without rereading the whole source, and what stops paraphrase from drifting into a different claim.

## Classifying claims

- `fact`: measured or observed, reported by a primary source or by two independent secondary ones.
- `estimate`: modelled, projected, extrapolated or surveyed with sampling; state the method if the source does.
- `opinion`: judgment, prediction without a model, marketing claim.
