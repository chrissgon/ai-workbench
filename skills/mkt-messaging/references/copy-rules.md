# Copy rules

Loaded at step 2 of `mkt-messaging`.

## Claims

- A claim is a sentence the reader could check. Write only checkable claims and give the check (a number with its method, a file that documents the behaviour, a citation).
- Superlatives ("the lightest", "the best") are claims against every alternative; they need a measurement of every alternative named in a research brief, or they are not made.
- "No", "zero" and "only" are strong claims; each needs a documented behaviour ("no CSS reset" is checkable in the stylesheet).

## Numbers

- Every number carries its unit, its method and its date somewhere on the page (a footnote is enough), and is reproduced in a PROOF.
- Round only as much as the method allows (a measured 2,922 B is "2.9 kB", not "under 3 kB" if it is 2,922 and not "3 kB" if precision matters to the claim).
- Numbers about the product come from a measurement run on the shipped build, never from the code's own comments or an older release.

## Tone

- Second person, present tense, active voice. One idea per sentence.
- The product's own vocabulary (its class names, its terms for its concepts) over marketing synonyms.
- Say what the product does not do when that is the point ("ships no font"); absence is a feature only when it is stated as one.
- No exclamation marks; no emoji in headlines.

## Structure of a landing page (default order)

1. Promise: headline, one sentence of body, install command, primary call to action.
2. The proof the reader most doubts (for a library: size with method; for a service: the result with a number).
3. How it works, shown live (the smallest complete example, with a demo the reader can touch).
4. Two to four features, each with its proof and its demo.
5. What it does not do, when that is a reason to choose it.
6. Migration or getting started for existing users, when they are an audience.
7. Install and links (repository, license, documentation).
